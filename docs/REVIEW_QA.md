# Questions a reviewer may ask

**Is the data real?** The structure and scale parameters can be calibrated from public data (rent listings, NYC violations, ...); the tenant records, interventions and outcomes are synthetic. No public dataset has tenant-level renewal outcomes after interventions.
**Then why believe the causal results?** On synthetic data we know the true effects, so we can *measure* estimator error (ATE error, PEHE, calibration, policy regret). We also show how it degrades under weaker effects and hidden confounding (`05_sensitivity.py`). On real data you would rely on the checks that need no ground truth: overlap, balance, refutations, E-values, an A/B pilot, off-policy evaluation.
**Why does a naive comparison say interventions hurt?** Confounding by indication: managers act on tenants who already look at risk. Adjustment (doubly-robust) and a randomised pilot fix this.
**What is "counterfactual" here?** The model's estimated renewal probability for the actions *not* taken. It is never observed for an individual; estimated under assumptions (no unmeasured confounding, overlap).
**Why is AUROC only ~0.74 and not 0.91?** Renewal is a Bernoulli draw; even the true probabilities only reach ~0.80 here. The 0.91 in the slides is another dataset.
**Why DR-learner and not just a T-learner?** DR combines an outcome model and a propensity model and stays consistent if either is right; in our runs S/T/DR give similar policy value - we report all of them.
**Where is the agent's autonomy?** It chooses tools/steps through a LangGraph state machine, but consequential actions require human approval and pass a policy guardrail. The wording can optionally be LLM-polished (numbers are never generated by the LLM).
**What if the manager overrides?** The approved action is executed and logged next to the recommended one (`overridden = true`).
**What are the limits?** Synthetic assumptions, weak individual-level effect identification, flat cost model, no discounting/vacancy dynamics, single-step decision (no sequential interactions between tenants beyond the budget).
